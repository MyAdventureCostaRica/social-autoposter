Rivers appearing opaque blue are the result of colloidal silica suspended in the water. These tiny mineral particles scatter blue light more effectively than other wavelengths. 

This scattering effect is a matter of light physics. It does not come from pigments or dyes in the riverbed. 

Transparency changes with the concentration of these particles. When the water is deep, the scattering becomes more pronounced to the human eye.
