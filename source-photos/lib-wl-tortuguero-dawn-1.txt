KNOWN FACTS (checked; the source of each is in brackets, for checking only, never for the caption):
- In 1955 Archie Carr, a zoologist from the University of Florida, began tagging green turtles at Tortuguero. Tagging has continued every year since: the longest continuous sea turtle monitoring programme anywhere. [source: https://conserveturtles.org/?p=418 ; https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s00227-005-0076-4]
- Carr wrote about turtle No. 3438, which he named Old Faithful: she was seen on this beach on 26 occasions in 7 nesting seasons between 1965 and 1982. [source: https://conserveturtles.org/?p=11628]
- The village once lived from turtle meat and eggs. Its guides now say a live green turtle brings in more money through tourism than a hunted one, and green turtle nests here rose 417% between 1971 and 2003. [source: https://conserveturtles.org/?p=11574 ; https://conserveturtles.org/?p=21299]
- Tortuguero is the most important nesting beach of the green turtle in the Western Hemisphere. [source: earlier library note]
- The hatchling's species is not confirmed: call it a sea-turtle hatchling.
