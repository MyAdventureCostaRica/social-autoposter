Before 1968, the mountain near La Fortuna was called Cerro Arenal, a forested hill. A documented expedition first reached its summit in 1937, but no scientists had studied it.

At about 7:30 a.m. on 29 July 1968, it exploded. A pyroclastic flow—a nube ardiente—destroyed the villages of Tabacón and Pueblo Nuevo, killing at least 87 people. Wind carried the ash as far as Tilarán, Liberia and Santa Cruz.

In one morning, what local families had known as a hill became the country's most active volcano.
