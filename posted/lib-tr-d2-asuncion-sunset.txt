In Costa Rica, páramo exists only above 3,000 m elevation.

Stunted bamboo, tough shrubs, and alpine grasses replace the dense oak forest of the lower slopes. The wind sweeps across open crests like Cerro Asunción, where the spine of the Talamanca range separates the Pacific and Caribbean watersheds.

Because of thin air on the continental divide, night temperatures drop near 0 °C before sun warms the ridges by mid-morning. It is an ecosystem that covers less than 1% of the country, restricted entirely to these high peaks.
