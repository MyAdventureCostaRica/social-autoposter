Each flower in an Ixora cluster hides its nectar at the bottom of a 30 mm tube.

That depth filters who feeds here. Only long-tongued butterflies and hawk moths reach the fluid directly. Smaller stingless bees cannot reach down the tube, so they chew through the petal base with their mandibles instead, taking the sugar without ever touching the anthers.

Ecologists call it nectar robbing. Across Costa Rican gardens and living fences, a few millimetres of plant tissue decide who pollinates the flower and who takes the nectar for free.
