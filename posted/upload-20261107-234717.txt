Along the dry Pacific coast, ground plants survive months without rain by holding water in thick, fleshy leaves.

When the trade winds arrive in January, surface moisture drops quickly. Grasses turn straw-coloured within weeks, and larger canopy trees shed foliage to limit water loss. Close to the soil, low succulents and creeping groundcovers keep flowering straight through the heat.

They supply pollen for resident bees when few other species can afford the moisture.
